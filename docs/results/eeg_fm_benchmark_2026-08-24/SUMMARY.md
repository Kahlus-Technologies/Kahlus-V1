## Headline

`kahlus_gru` ranks **first at six of nine horizons** (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 128), second at one
(32), and third at two (64, 256) where the margins are 0.002 and 0.0005 — statistical ties.

On the paired subject-level test that actually decides ranking, Kahlus is **significantly
better than the best external entrant at five of eight claim-eligible horizons** (2, 4, 8,
16, 128 after Holm correction) and **statistically tied at the other three**. It never
significantly loses at any horizon.

## The negative control fired: pretraining bought nothing

Every external model ran twice — real pretrained weights, and a random-init twin of the
identical architecture. The twin matched or beat the pretrained model in **25 of 72**
model×condition×horizon cells, including EEGPT under full fine-tuning at 7 of 9 horizons
(its twin is *strictly better* at all 7: h=1, 2, 4, 8, 64, 128, 256) and LaBraM at
h=2, 64, 128.

Per plan section 5 this is a pre-registered kill criterion: it blocks the ranking claim
rather than annotating it. Two readings are consistent with the data and this experiment
cannot separate them:

1. Pretraining genuinely does not transfer to single-channel next-sample forecasting at
   ~1 s of context. These are masked-infilling classifiers evaluated off-task.
2. The adapters handicap the pretrained weights, so pretrained and random weights are
   equalised by construction — a bottlenecked representation makes the twin control
   uninformative rather than damning.

An earlier revision said the evidence "leans toward (1)". The PR #80 adversarial review
traced (and we have since reproduced) four structural bottlenecks that push the frozen
arms toward (2), beyond the previously self-flagged items (EEGPT's monopolar `FPZ` proxy
for the bipolar Fpz-Cz derivation, LaBraM's untrained `fc_norm` under the frozen probe,
and 1.0–1.25 s contexts versus 4 s+ pretraining):

- **EEGPT's encoder is temporal-order-invariant as adapted.** Its transformer blocks
  attend only within each 64-sample patch (time patches are folded into the batch axis;
  RoPE lives in the pretraining predictor, which we drop), and the adapter mean-pools the
  patch tokens. Verified numerically: permuting the patches in time leaves the pre-pool
  tokens bit-identical. A representation that cannot see temporal order cannot forecast,
  which is consistent with its twin matching or beating it at 7 of 9 full-ft horizons.
- **BIOT's representation deletes phase.** Upstream's STFT returns magnitudes only, and at
  this context length (200 samples, `n_fft=200`, `hop=100`, `center=False`) the whole
  context becomes a single phase-free magnitude spectrum. Next-sample regression needs
  phase; BIOT's flat 1.10–1.14 rows and failed corruption controls are "cannot represent
  the task", not "weak results".
- **CBraMod's pretrained attention is a no-op here.** One channel × one 200-sample patch
  means both criss-cross attention axes see a single token (softmax weight ≡ 1.0,
  measured), and only 0.75% of its 19×30-grid positional-conv kernel touches real data —
  the rest convolves zero padding. Its projections still apply, but the pretrained
  attention patterns are dead weight.
- **Per-window FFT resampling corrupts exactly the samples that matter (undisclosed until
  now).** Resampling each 127-sample context independently produces circular-extension
  ringing concentrated at the window edges: measured on 1/f (EEG-like) signals, ~13–21%
  of signal power in the last 4 resampled samples (~50–74% in the very last sample),
  against a clean resample of the unbroken series. Length alignment trims from the front,
  so the corruption lands on the most recent — most predictive — history. The 100 Hz
  incumbent skips resampling entirely and eats none of this.

The fairest FM datapoint in the run is therefore CBraMod full fine-tune, which retrains
everything and whose attention is degenerate only in its *pretrained pattern*, not its
capacity: it reaches 0.335 at h = 1 vs Kahlus's 0.311, is the best rival at every short
horizon, and beats its own random twin at all 9 horizons — so some pretraining benefit
does survive there. LaBraM full-ft similarly learns the task (0.337 at h = 1). The
architecture-can-learn-it observation stands, but for the frozen arms and for EEGPT the
bottleneck evidence means this run cannot say pretraining failed to transfer — only that
no pretraining benefit survived these adapters. Resolving (1) vs (2) needs Lane B
(multi-channel, native context, no per-window resampling), which is out of scope here.

BIOT is separately degenerate: 1.10–1.14 flat at every horizon, identical to its twin, and
it failed both corruption controls. Its rows are non-results, not weak results.

## Gate verdict: claim blocked (by design)

`scientific_claim_allowed = false`. What passed and what did not:

| passed | failed |
|---|---|
| `powered` (27 subjects, every CI within the ratified MDE 0.10) | `controls_pass` (BIOT label-shuffle + time-shift) |
| `protocol_ran_as_declared` | `beat_best_baseline` (ties at h=32, 64, 256 are not wins) |
| `single_test_look_per_family` | `no_grid_edge_selected` |
| `tuning_budget_symmetric` | random-twin kill criterion (above) |
| `determinism_enabled` | `cluster_permutation_null` not implemented |
| `checkpoint_provenance_verified` | `subject_identity_probe` not implemented |
| `index_audit_passed`, `normalization_causal` | |
| `harness_sanity_passed`, `incumbent_ladder_complete` | |

`beat_best_baseline` demands a win at *every* claim-eligible horizon and scores a tie as a
non-win. The gate refusing is therefore not "Kahlus lost" — it is the gate declining to
certify a ranking claim while a degenerate entrant sits in the table, two planned controls
are unimplemented, and three horizons are ties.

Scope notes on the passed keys (PR #80 review):

- `powered` certifies the best-rival CIs against the 0.10 MDE; some non-best-rival CIs
  are wider (BIOT at h = 16 has width 0.141). "Every CI within the MDE" in the headline
  refers to the comparisons that decide the ranking.
- The label-shuffle and time-shift corruption controls ran at h = 2 only, not at all
  eight claim-eligible horizons.
- `tuning_budget_symmetric` holds per entrant row (14 configs, one test look each). Each
  FM fields two ranked rows (frozen + full_ft), so per *model* the FMs got 28 validation
  configs and two test looks against the GRU's 14 + 1 — an asymmetry that favours the
  FMs, so it cannot have inflated the incumbent's ranking.
- `checkpoint_provenance_verified` rests on strict `load_state_dict` plus the SHA-256
  manifest; the EEGPT row has `load_report_captured: false` (the load report is a count,
  not a key-by-key report — a capture gap, not a verification gap).

To convert this into a certifiable claim: implement the two missing controls, either drop
BIOT or give it a fair context, widen the grid axis that hit an edge, and restrict the
claim to the horizons where signal exists (2–16, plus 128).
