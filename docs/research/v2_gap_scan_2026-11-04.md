Sleep Uncertainty Quantification: Methods and Gaps
Sleep staging already has practical uncertainty methods, but conformal guarantees for staging, arousals, and apnea remain largely an unvalidated research direction in the supplied literature. The central distinction is between identifying unreliable predictions, calibrating their probabilities, and guaranteeing prediction-set coverage: these are different objectives.
1. What Is Established
Sleep-specific evidence is strongest for uncertainty-guided staging and selective expert review, rather than formally guaranteed prediction sets. In real-world wearable EEG from older adults, uncertainty-guided processing increased five-stage accuracy from 63.9% to 71.2%, although specialist review without wearable-specific training was ineffective 
Heremans 2024 .

3. Calibration and Modality Transfer
Probability calibration requires more than showing that rejected epochs contain more errors. The direct comparison of hypnodensity methods evaluates calibration, whereas many ensemble studies primarily report discrimination and agreement

Evaluation design can dominate apparent reliability. A subject-independent evaluation reduced accuracy from 91.2% to 82.4% and N1 sensitivity from 72.52% to 27.15% compared with subject-non-independent testing

Köksal 2026 . Subject-level splitting is therefore an explicitly identified requirement in ECG-to-PPG transfer research

5. Transferable Conformal Methods
Conformal prediction supplies prediction sets with statistical coverage rather than requiring a single stage label 
Zhou 2024 . However, standard validity relies on exchangeability, which temporal drift and repeated within-patient observations complicate 
Barber 2022 Lee 2023 . These are methodological candidates for sleep research, not established sleep applications.
Hierarchical conformal methods address grouped or repeated measurements under hierarchical exchangeability 
Lee 2023 . This is distinct from hierarchical forecast reconciliation, which concerns outcomes related by linear aggregation and should not automatically be interpreted as patient-cluster handling 
Principato 2024 . Weighted methods address distribution drift, while group-weighted methods address particular group-driven covariate shifts; estimated shift weights introduce additional uncertainty 
Barber 2022 Bhattacharyya 2024 .
Marginal coverage is not patient-specific protection. General finite-sample conditional coverage is impossible without restrictions, although prespecified subgroup guarantees are available 
Gibbs 2023 . Conditional-coverage diagnostics can help expose localized undercoverage hidden by pooled results 
Braun 2025 .
For sequential outputs, EnbPI and sequential predictive conformal inference offer dependence-aware approaches, with assumptions and asymptotic coverage qualifications rather than unrestricted guarantees 
Xu 2020 Xu 2022 . Online risk control and anytime-valid approaches address different monitoring settings and guarantee targets 
Feldman 2022 Hultberg 2026 .
Conformal risk control is particularly relevant to missed-event losses because it extends beyond label miscoverage to monotone expected losses, including false-negative-rate examples 
Angelopoulos 2022 . Selection-aware procedures matter when only small prediction sets are retained, because ordinary coverage need not survive selection 
Gazin 2024 . Adaptive coverage policies and conditional risk adaptation offer further possibilities, but their supplied demonstrations are outside sleep medicine 
Gauthier 2025 Luo 2025 .

The field of closed-loop sleep neurostimulation has matured rapidly around reactive triggering — delivering stimuli the moment a target brain state is detected — but the question of whether forecasting an impending state transition and acting before it occurs yields superior outcomes remains almost entirely unanswered. 4. Discussion
The reactive closed-loop paradigm is technically mature for auditory stimulation: end-to-end latencies below 100–150 ms are routinely achieved, phase-targeting errors are on the order of tens of degrees for SOs, and ambulatory dry-EEG devices replicate lab-grade performance at home

5. Conclusion
Closed-loop sleep interventions have established a robust reactive paradigm for auditory stimulation, with emerging evidence for thermal and vibrotactile modalities, but the field has not yet demonstrated that forecasting an impending state transition and acting before it occurs improves outcomes over detecting and reacting to it. The engineering infrastructure for forecasting now exists — accurate arousal predictors, real-time stage forecasters, and pre-sleep brain-state classifiers — but none has been coupled to a prospective intervention trial. Translating this infrastructure into clinical benefit requires head-to-head trials, adaptive controllers, and validated wearable platforms.

Open Research Questions
Future work must move from demonstrating that forecasting is accurate to demonstrating that acting on a forecast changes sleep outcomes.

	RL provides principled adaptive optimization but has not been validated in human sleep stimulation

Forecasting spontaneous cortical arousals before they occur is an emerging but largely unresolved challenge. The literature overwhelmingly addresses detection concurrent with or after arousal onset, and only a handful of studies have explicitly tested prediction horizons ahead of the event.

1. Introduction
Cortical arousals are transient intrusions of wakefulness into sleep, defined by the AASM as abrupt EEG frequency shifts lasting at least 3 seconds following 10 continuous seconds of sleep 
Huo 2023 Kucukosmanoglu 2025 . Frequent arousals fragment sleep, impair daytime function, and are associated with cardiovascular dysfunction 
Huo 2023 Kucukosmanoglu 2025 . The gold standard for arousal identification remains polysomnography, which is costly and impractical for home monitoring 
Kucukosmanoglu 2025 Kucukosmanoglu 2025 . This has motivated a large body of work using cardiac signals — ECG, HRV, and PPG — as surrogate markers, since most cortical arousals are accompanied by autonomic nervous system activation observable in the heart rate signal 
Li 2020 Huo 2023 .
The critical distinction motivating this review is between detection (identifying an arousal as it occurs or has just occurred) and forecasting (predicting an arousal before it begins, providing a lead time for intervention). The vast majority of published algorithms are detectors, not forecasters 
Baker 2025 . Understanding what physiological signals precede arousal onset — and by how long — is the prerequisite for any genuine forecasting system. Cardiac acceleration has been shown to precede EEG-defined arousal onset by several seconds in healthy children and adults 
Baumert 2010 Sforza 2000 , and heart-to-brain coupling increases significantly in the pre-arousal period 
Gumussu 2026 . Whether these precursors are reliable enough to support prospective forecasting, and at what horizon, is the central open question.

The search strategy combined targeted queries on pre-onset arousal prediction, cardiovascular precursors of cortical arousals, ECG/HRV/PPG-based arousal detection, and the PhysioNet 2018 Challenge dataset, supplemented by citation tracing from key forecasting and detection papers and author-level searches.

3.2 Physiological Precursors: What Cardiac Signals Reveal Before Arousal
The biological basis for forecasting rests on the observation that cardiac activation consistently precedes EEG-defined cortical arousal onset by several seconds. In healthy children, R-R interval shortening (heart rate acceleration) preceded cortical arousal onset by 3.5 seconds during stage 2 sleep and 5.0 seconds during REM sleep, replicated across two polysomnography nights 
Baumert 2010 . In healthy adults, heart rate always increased before microarousal and phases of transitory activation onset, associated with a concurrent rise in delta, theta, and fast EEG activity 
Sforza 2000 . Convergent cross-mapping analysis confirms that heart-to-brain coupling increases significantly in all EEG frequency bands pre-arousal and recovers post-arousal, indicating autonomic-cortical signaling as a hallmark of arousal dynamics 
Gumussu 2026 .
These precursors are not universal. Autonomic activations can occur without any cortical arousal, and 81% of ECG-detected autonomic activations not associated with cortical arousals are linked instead to leg movements (38%) or respiratory events (43%) 
Olsen 2018 . PPG responses to arousals also lag EEG onset rather than precede it: the delay is longest after apnea-related arousals and shortest after spontaneous arousals 
Luukinen 2024 . This means PPG, while useful for detection, may be structurally disadvantaged for pre-onset forecasting of spontaneous events.
3.3 The One Explicit Forecasting Study: DeepArousal-Net
DeepArousal-Net is the only identified study that explicitly frames its task as forecasting non-apneic arousals before onset rather than detecting them concurrently 
Baker 2025 . The model uses a multi-block CNN + Bidirectional LSTM architecture applied to multichannel PSG signals including EEG, ECG, EOG, EMG, oxygen saturation, and airflow 
Baker 2025 . At a 30-second prediction horizon, it achieved accuracy 81.31%, sensitivity 71.23%, and specificity 81.90% 
Baker 2025 . The model outperformed traditional time-series prediction methods and is described as a significant advance over post-event detection systems 
Baker 2025 .
Two critical caveats apply. First, the model relies on full PSG including EEG — it is not a cardiac-only or wearable-ready system. Second, the 30-second horizon is the only one tested; no study has yet demonstrated reliable forecasting at longer horizons (e.g., 1–5 minutes) for spontaneous cortical arousals using any signal modality.
3.4 Detection Performance as a Ceiling Reference
Because no cardiac-only forecasting study exists for spontaneous arousals, detection performance provides the practical ceiling against which any future forecasting system must be measured. ECG-based deep learning detection of arousals from single-lead ECG achieved AUROC 0.93 and AUPRC 0.62 on the MESA dataset 
Li 2020 . A CNN trained on heart rate alone (RR intervals) achieved AUROC 0.91 and AUPRC 0.67 across 1,083 PSGs 
Lockyer 2021 . A 12-layer CNN on the Sleep Heart Health Study achieved Cohen's κ of 0.47 and AUPRC 0.54 using only RR intervals 
Ehrlich 2022 . PPG-based detection of respiratory arousals achieved AUROC 0.91 with sensitivity 68.1% and specificity 95.2% 
Karmakar 2014 . A PPG-only collaborative learning framework (CSleep) estimated arousal index with mean bias error of 4.8 events/hour 
Li 2024 . Multimodal wearable detection using accelerometry, gyroscope, and PPG achieved ROC-AUC 0.94, though movement features dominated and HRV contributed comparatively little, partly due to insufficient PPG sampling rates 
Kucukosmanoglu 2025 Kucukosmanoglu 2025 .
An important analogy comes from the HRV-based prediction of vasovagal syncope, where PPG-derived HRV maintained AUROC 0.91 at a 5-minute lead time before syncope onset 
Lee 2026 . Similarly, HRV-based drowsiness detection preceded behavioral manifestations by 5–8 minutes and crash events by 6.8 ± 2.3 minutes 
Vaussenat 2026 . These analogous domains demonstrate that cardiac-signal forecasting at minute-scale horizons is achievable for other autonomic events, but has not yet been demonstrated for spontaneous cortical arousals specifically.

4. Discussion
The evidence base for forecasting spontaneous cortical arousals before onset is thin but not empty. The physiological foundation is solid: cardiac acceleration reliably precedes EEG arousal onset by 3–5 seconds in both children and adults 
Baumert 2010 Sforza 2000 , and the coupling between autonomic and cortical systems during the pre-arousal window is directionally consistent 
Gumussu 2026 . However, the gap between this physiological observation and a deployable forecasting system is large. The only study that explicitly tests forecasting — DeepArousal-Net at 30 seconds — requires full PSG including EEG, which defeats the purpose of a cardiac-only wearable approach 
Baker 2025 . No study has tested cardiac-only (ECG, HRV, or PPG) forecasting of spontaneous arousals at any horizon with prospective validation.
A key limitation across the literature is the conflation of detection and forecasting. Many studies that label their output as "prediction" are in fact classifying windows that contain the arousal event, not windows that precede it 
Kuo 2023 . The wearable multimodal study that observed "pre-arousal predictions appearing within one to two minutes of labeled events" was observing temporal misalignment in a detection model, not a validated forecasting horizon 
Kucukosmanoglu 2025 Kucukosmanoglu 2025 . Similarly, the HRV-based CPAP adjustment study frames its output as "proactive" but targets pressure adjustment timing rather than arousal onset prediction 
Kuo 2025 . The HRV-based sleep onset forecasting study using ARIMA and critical slowing down detected early warning signals in 92% of cases within a 10-minute window, but this targets sleep onset, not intra-sleep arousals 
Demareva 2023 .
A further structural challenge is that spontaneous arousals — those not triggered by apnea, leg movements, or other identifiable stimuli — are the hardest subtype to forecast because they lack a reliable physiological precursor chain. Respiratory-effort-related arousals have a detectable respiratory signature that precedes them 
Finnsson 2025 Ghassemi 2018 , but spontaneous arousals constitute only a small fraction of the PhysioNet 2018 Challenge dataset (0.4% of non-RERA target arousals) 
Rad 2019 . The autonomic response to spontaneous arousals is also weaker and shorter-latency than to respiratory arousals, with PPG frequency responses less intense after spontaneous than respiratory events 
Luukinen 2024 . HRV reliability during disrupted sleep is generally maintained across N2, SWS, and REM 
Kerkering 2022 , but the signal-to-noise ratio for pre-arousal HRV changes is low, and PPG signal quality drops from 0.818 to 0.488 during arousal events in wearable recordings 
Kucukosmanoglu 2025 .

5. Conclusion
The literature establishes that cardiac signals carry genuine pre-arousal information — heart rate accelerates 3–5 seconds before EEG-defined cortical arousal onset — and that deep learning can forecast non-apneic arousals 30 seconds ahead using full PSG. However, no study has demonstrated cardiac-only (ECG, HRV, or PPG) forecasting of spontaneous cortical arousals at any horizon with prospective validation, leaving the core question of this review unanswered by current evidence.

1) FORECASTING SPONTANEOUS AROUSALS FROM ECG/HRV/PPG

Key papers:
- Cardiac activation during arousal in humans (Sforza, 2000, Clinical Neurophysiology). Heart rate always rose before micro-arousal onset. This is the physiological basis for forecasting.
- Cardiorespiratory response to spontaneous cortical arousals (Baumert, 2010, J Sleep Res). In healthy children, heartbeats sped up 3.5 s before arousal onset in N2 and 5.0 s before in REM.
- A deep learning-based algorithm for detection of cortical arousal during sleep (Li, 2020, Sleep). Single-lead ECG on MESA reached AUROC 0.93 and AUPRC 0.62. This is detection, not forecasting, but it's the ceiling a forecaster has to approach.
- DeepArousal-Net (Yousefi Jamarani, 2025 online / 2026 print, IEEE TBME). <citation refs="8XRIhao5THa3XoudDp1L-">Forecast non-apneic arousals 30 s ahead at 81.31% accuracy, 71.23% sensitivity and 81.90% specificity</citation>. Its inputs <citation refs="lzakMh_MvQ_q6r2UlTCZR,YqfCdmYcsgtL7PfrJAQTv">include EEG, ECG, EOG, EMG, SpO2 and airflow</citation>.
- Variation in the PPG response to arousal (Luukinen, 2024, IEEE JTEHM). The PPG response lags EEG onset, with the shortest lag for spontaneous arousals. PPG may be at a disadvantage for pre-onset forecasting.
- Automatic ECG-based detection of autonomic arousals (Olsen, 2018, Sleep). Many autonomic activations happen without a cortical arousal and come from leg movements or respiratory events. Expect false alarms from these.
- Related: Gliske 2025 (IEEE NER) forecast awakenings about 2.6 min ahead from subthalamic LFP in Parkinson's, beating chance in 3 of 4 patients. Bahrami 2022 (Cardiovasc Eng Technol) forecast apnea from single-lead ECG.

The gap: no study forecasts spontaneous cortical arousals from cardiac signals alone (ECG, HRV or PPG) at any horizon.
- The only real forecaster uses EEG and runs no autonomic-only ablation.
- Nobody has swept the horizon (5 s to 2 min), tested on a different cohort, or broken results out by arousal subtype.
- Many papers that say "prediction" are actually classifying windows that contain the event.
- PhysioNet 2018 framed the problem as detection, so benchmarks reward detection.

2) REAL-TIME CLOSED-LOOP INTERVENTIONS

Key papers:
- Auditory closed-loop stimulation of the sleep slow oscillation enhances memory (Ngo, 2013, Neuron). Clicks timed to the slow-oscillation up-state boosted slow oscillations and memory. This set up the reactive approach the field still uses.
- Performance of an ambulatory dry-EEG device for auditory closed-loop stimulation at home (Debellemaniere, 2018, Front Hum Neurosci).
  - Real-time N3 detection: sensitivity 0.70, specificity 0.90.
  - Phase error was 45 ± 52 degrees.
  - Delta power rose 43.9% after the first stimulus.
- CLAS of slow-wave sleep in chronic insomnia (Dudysova, 2024, J Sleep Res). 58% of patients got too few stimuli under thresholds built for healthy sleepers. There was no change in arousal count or sleep structure.
- Effect of closed-loop vibration stimulation on sleep quality for poor sleepers (Kwon, 2024, Front Neurosci). Vibration timed to heart rate reduced wake after sleep onset (WASO) and raised N3 delta power. It was a single crossover study.
- Adaptive thermal regulation (Kim, 2025, Healthcare) and Eight Sleep stage-based autopilot (Ediger, 2025, SLEEP abstract). Mattress temperature changed by sleep stage improved sleep efficiency and REM.
- Acoustic stimulation time-locked to apnea onset (Waeber, 2020, Sleep Medicine). Desaturation amplitude dropped 30% and duration 40%, and patients noticed only 6.8% of stimuli. This is the closest thing to stimulation triggered by an upcoming event.
- Related: Sardooeinasab 2026 (SLEEP) found offline that combining slow-oscillation phase with heart-rate phase roughly doubles the evoked slow-oscillation amplitude (about 38 vs 18 µV). It has not been tested in a live loop.

The gap: no randomized trial compares forecast-triggered stimulation with reactive stimulation in any modality, so the benefit of lead time is purely theoretical.
- Thermal and vibration systems use fixed schedules with no feedback-driven controller.
- No CLAS system uses a predicted arousal to hold off stimulation. Current systems pause only after an arousal is detected.
- Personal thresholds for clinical groups are mostly unexplored.

3) LOW-COST AND OPEN-HARDWARE WEARABLE EEG VS PSG

Key papers:
- The Dreem Headband compared to polysomnography (Arnal, 2020, Sleep). With 25 adults, the headband hit 83.5% accuracy vs 86.4% for experts. N1 F1 was only 46.5%.
- Assessing a portable EEG sleep monitor against level 1 PSG (Lanthier, 2025, Sleep Advances). Muse-S reached five-stage kappa 0.76, but 9 of 56 recordings were unusable.
- Machine learning detection of arousals from at-home sleep EEG device (Furbass, 2026, SLEEP abstract). Waveband dry EEG had arousal-index ICC 0.89 but 30-s epoch kappa only 0.59. This is the only headband result on arousals against PSG that Consensus found.
- Automatic scoring of sleep stages and cortical arousals using two forehead electrodes (Popovic, 2013, J Sleep Res). The arousal index was accurate in healthy adults, but there are no event-matching metrics.
- Systematic comparison of high-end and low-cost EEG amplifiers (Knierim, 2023, Sensors). OpenBCI Cyton+Daisy had noise similar to a research amplifier but worse timing. This is an acquisition benchmark, not a sleep validation.
- Wearable EEG sleep monitoring devices meta-analysis (Markov, 2025, npj Biomedical Innovations). Across 43 PSG validation studies, N3 was the most reliable stage and N1 the weakest.

The gap: Consensus's coverage grid found zero papers on arousal index or event-level arousals for Dreem, Muse, OpenBCI or ADS1299-based devices.
- OpenBCI and ADS1299 work tests signal quality or public datasets, not simultaneous overnight PSG.
- Nobody reports event-matched precision, recall and timing error, or failed-night rates under self-application.

4) CONFORMAL PREDICTION AND CALIBRATED UNCERTAINTY

Key papers:
- SleepTransformer (Phan, 2022, IEEE TBME). It flags epochs for review by predictive entropy. This is the standard uncertainty baseline.
- DeepSleepNet-Lite (Fiorillo, 2021, IEEE TNSRE). Monte Carlo dropout gives uncertainty on single-channel EEG.
- Hypnodensity based on multiple expert scorers (Bakker, 2022, Sleep). The model's stage-probability distribution matched multi-scorer distributions (ICC 0.91), so stage ambiguity is a real target to predict.
- U-PASS (Heremans, 2024, Computers in Biology and Medicine). Uncertainty-guided training and expert deferral raised accuracy from 75% to 85% in elderly apnea patients.
- Direct quantification of uncertainty in automatic sleep staging (Vainikka, 2025, IEEE TBME). Accuracy reached 92% after dropping the 20% most uncertain epochs.
- SCRIB (Lin, 2022, AAAI). A set classifier with class-specific risk bounds, tested on EEG sleep staging. It is the closest thing to conformal in sleep.
- Related: Chatterjee 2026 (arXiv, EEG seizures) found <citation refs="0KU8NGtNYWc8dxYFiE-d4">personalized calibration improved coverage by over 20 percentage points at similar set sizes</citation> under patient shift.

The gap: Consensus's grid showed zero papers on conformal coverage or probability calibration for arousal detection or apnea assessment, and none on conformal for peripheral-signal staging.
- No sleep study compares temperature scaling, isotonic regression and conformal methods head to head.
- Epochs within a night are dependent, which breaks the exchangeability assumption most conformal methods rely on. Hierarchical, weighted and online conformal methods exist but haven't been applied to sleep.
- Dropping uncertain epochs leaves holes in the hypnogram, and whole-night metrics like AHI and arousal index then lose their meaning. No one has solved this.

RANKED PROJECT IDEAS (8 A100s, MESA/SHHS, 6 months)

1. Cardiac-only arousal forecasting benchmark. Forecast arousals from ECG (SHHS, MESA) and PPG (MESA) at horizons of 5, 15, 30, 60 and 120 s.
   - Baselines:
     - An EEG-inclusive model as the upper bound.
     - A horizon-0 detector, to show how much is really forecasting.
     - A simple model based only on time since the last arousal and current sleep stage. This one matters because arousals cluster, and a model could just learn that.
   - Train on SHHS and test on MESA, then the reverse.
   - Report results by subtype (spontaneous, respiratory, limb) with event-level F1 and false alarms per hour.
   - This targets gap 1 directly. Likely venues: SLEEP, IEEE JBHI or npj Digital Medicine, with an ML4H workshop paper as a fast first step.
2. Calibrated arousal forecasts with an alarm-budget guarantee. Wrap the idea 1 model in conformal risk control so the miss rate stays under a target.
   - Calibrate by subject or hierarchically, and use weighted conformal for the SHHS-to-MESA shift.
   - Report coverage by age, AHI, sex and race. MESA's diversity is the selling point.
   - It reuses idea 1's pipeline, so both fit in 6 months. Targets gap 4. Venues: CHIL, ML4H, or JBHI.
3. Offline "would-have-stimulated" study. Replay PSG nights through a standard CLAS trigger and through a version that holds off when ECG arousal risk is high.
   - Measure how many stimuli would land in the seconds before spontaneous arousals.
   - Weigh lost stimulation opportunity against arousal risk avoided, overall and in insomnia-like and high-AHI subgroups.
   - It can't show a causal benefit, but it's the design groundwork for the missing forecast-vs-reactive trial (gap 2). Venues: J Neural Eng, EMBC, or a SLEEP abstract.
4. NSRR-wide heart-and-breathing foundation model for event forecasting. Pretrain on SHHS, MESA, MrOS, CFS and so on (8 A100s is enough). Fine-tune for arousal and stage-transition forecasting, and measure label efficiency and cross-cohort drop. It has the highest ceiling but the most competition, since PFTSleep and SleepFM-style models exist and you could get scooped.
5. Event-level stress test for low-cost montages. Degrade PSG EEG to mimic a cheap headband: single frontal-ish channel, ADS1299-like noise, lower sampling rate, dry-electrode artifacts. Then measure arousal-event detection loss with matched-event metrics, and propose a reporting standard. It's least novel without real device data. A small OpenBCI plus simultaneous PSG pilot (5 to 10 nights, needs IRB) would make it much stronger.

Practical notes for 1 and 2:
- NSRR doesn't label arousals as spontaneous. You'd have to call an arousal spontaneous when no respiratory event or leg movement is nearby.
- As far as I know, SHHS has no leg EMG and no raw PPG waveform, so in SHHS "spontaneous" can only mean non-respiratory, and PPG work has to use MESA. Check the EDF headers before committing.
- Human scorers only agree on arousals at about kappa 0.56 to 0.60 (Alvarez-Estevez 2018), which caps any arousal metric.
- Use subject-level splits throughout. Your earlier search found Aggul 2026, where accuracy fell from 95.6% to 61.0% once participants were kept separate.
