Consensus Meter: not shown (the question it would have scored was "Do autonomic signals improve pre-onset cortical arousal forecasting beyond EEG baselines in MESA, SHHS, or MrOS?").
Summary: Heart and pulse signals do change before arousals, but nobody has shown they forecast arousals better than EEG across MESA, SHHS and MrOS. The evidence is strong that ECG can detect arousals after the fact. It is moderate that heart and pulse changes come before arousals, and weak that anyone can forecast them ahead of time. No study combines all three of these: real forecasting, a matched EEG-only comparison, and testing on a cohort the model never saw. That is the gap.
Key findings:
- Detection isn't forecasting. ECG detects arousals well after the fact (MESA: AUROC 0.93, AUPRC 0.62). An earlier ECG algorithm used the 5 heartbeats after arousal onset. FullSleepNet got AUPRC 0.70 on SHHS+MESA, and CAISR got 0.67 to 0.85 on external data. None of these are advance warnings.
- Precursors exist. Heart activity can start rising about 1 second before the EEG changes. A small nap study (n=15) saw pulse and breathing changes in the 30 seconds before arousals. But heart-rate bursts happen about as often without a cortical arousal as with one, so the signal isn't specific.
- The only direct forecasting result is DeepArousal-Net (IEEE TBME 2025). It forecasts 30 seconds ahead with 81.3% accuracy, 71.2% sensitivity and 81.9% specificity. But it uses EEG plus ECG and other channels together. It doesn't test autonomic signals against EEG alone, and it doesn't test MESA to SHHS transfer.
- No PPG study forecasts arousals at all. MESA PPG quality also gets worse around arousals, which can skew results.
- Foundation models are useful as feature extractors, not as arousal forecasters. SleepFM held SHHS out of pretraining and then transferred to it, and PSG-MAE was cross-validated on MESA. Benchmarks show specialist models still compete well, and the rankings change from dataset to dataset.
- Cross-cohort: training on both MESA and SHHS is not the same as training on MESA and testing on SHHS. Scoring rules, EEG referencing and patient mix all differ between cohorts.
- Leakage: splitting by segment instead of by person inflates results. One audit found 95.6% accuracy with random windows vs 61.0% with people kept separate (ADHD), and 92.0% vs 56.2% (Parkinson's). Overlapping windows are only a problem when the same person ends up in both training and test sets.
- Suggested safeguards: keep all of a person's nights in one split, tune and select features inside the training data only, track which cohorts each model was pretrained on, and check labeling windows. One pipeline stretched labels 2 seconds before onset.
- Experiment Consensus proposed: compare EEG-only, autonomic-only and combined models with a fixed input cutoff and forecast gap. Report false alarms per hour, lead-time distribution and calibration, with uncertainty estimated by resampling participants.

Summary: Large self-supervised and transformer sleep models do carry over to other sleep-study cohorts, but performance usually drops because of differences in demographics and recording setups. Training on several cohorts is what makes them generalize.
Key findings:
- PFTSleep (Fox 2024) was trained on 8,444 SHHS recordings and tested on 2,055 MESA recordings. Performance dropped, worst for deep sleep (N3 AUPRC fell from 0.75 to 0.45), and the authors blame the age difference between cohorts.
- SLEEPYLAND (Rossi 2025): models trained only on SHHS fall apart on other datasets. Training on multiple cohorts plus an ensemble (SOMNUS) holds up.
- Fox 2025: a model pretrained on HSP, MrOS and MESA, then applied to SHHS, predicted cardiovascular death at AUROC 0.74.
- Luna 2025: pretraining on 7 NSRR cohorts improved stage decoding from pulse and breathing signals in MESA by about 10%.
- Models pretrained on full sleep studies adapt to ear-EEG, single-channel headbands and REM sleep behavior disorder patients with little fine-tuning. Channel-agnostic AnySleep trains on SHHS and MESA and still works with whatever electrodes are available.
